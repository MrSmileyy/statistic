Knuth recursive formulas, multi-hacker simulation, and unbiasedness properties of sample estimators

1. Core Logic & Knuth's Formulation

In sequential or streaming variance computation, using the textbook formula Var = (∑ x2 - (∑ x)2/n) / (n - 1) is highly vulnerable to catastrophic cancellation due to subtraction in standard IEEE 754 floating-point arithmetic.

B.P. Welford's algorithm (1962), refined by Donald Knuth (The Art of Computer Programming, Vol. 2), solves this by updating the mean and the sum of squared deviations (M2 = SS) in a single pass with O(1) space using exactly 3 lines of code:

// Knuth/Welford update block (3 pure lines) const delta = x - mean; mean += delta / count; M2 += delta * (x - mean); // Unbiased sample variance (Bessel's correction n - 1): const sampleVariance = count > 1 ? M2 / (count - 1) : 0;

Analytical Recurrence Relations:

Deviation from previous mean: δk = xk - μk-1
Mean update: μk = μk-1 + δk / k
SS (M2) update: M2,k = M2,k-1 + δk ⋅ (xk - μk)
Unbiased estimator of σ2: s2k = M2,k / (k - 1)

Technical note: In the third line, the term (x - mean) uses the newly updated mean μk. The algebraic identity δk ⋅ (xk - μk) = (xk - μk-1)(xk - μk) guarantees maximum numerical precision without requiring two passes over the data.

Theoretical Deep Dive: Welford, Knuth, and Sampling

1. Welford's Algorithm and Knuth's Formulation

The standard algebraic textbook formula for the sum of squared deviations is SS = ∑ xi2 - (∑ xi)2 / n. Although mathematically equivalent to ∑ (xi - μ)2, in finite-precision IEEE 754 floating-point arithmetic it subtracts two huge numbers of nearly equal magnitude when the data mean is large relative to its variance. This causes catastrophic cancellation, potentially yielding zero or even negative variances.

B.P. Welford (1962) introduced a single-pass recursive method refined by Donald Knuth. Denoting μk as the mean of the first k elements and M2,k as the sum of squared deviations:
 δk = xk - μk-1
 μk = μk-1 + δk / k
 M2,k = M2,k-1 + δk ⋅ (xk - μk)
The unbiased sample variance is then simply s2 = M2,k / (k - 1).

2. Bessel's Correction (Divisor n - 1)

When estimating population variance σ2 from a sample, we replace the unknown true mean μ with the sample mean X̄. Because X̄ minimizes the sum of squared distances from the sample points, ∑ (Xi - X̄)2 systematically underestimates the dispersion around μ:
 E[∑ (Xi - X̄)2 / n] = ((n - 1) / n) ⋅ σ2
Multiplying by Bessel's factor n / (n - 1) yields the unbiased estimator:
 E[S2] = E[M2 / (n - 1)] = σ2.

3. Score Distributions & Exact Moments

  • Discrete Uniform U{0, 10}: Each hacker scores an integer uniformly between 0 and 10 (K = 11 outcomes with probability 1/11). Mean μ = (0 + 10) / 2 = 5.0; Variance σ2 = (K2 - 1) / 12 = 120 / 12 = 10.0 (σ = √10 ≈ 3.1623).
  • Binomial Bin(10, 0.5): Each hacker performs 10 independent Bernoulli trials with p = 0.5. Mean μ = 10 ⋅ 0.5 = 5.0; Variance σ2 = 10 ⋅ 0.5 ⋅ 0.5 = 2.50 (σ ≈ 1.5811).

4. Law of Large Numbers & Central Limit Theorem

By the Central Limit Theorem, the sample mean X̄n of n i.i.d. random variables converges in distribution to a normal distribution:
 X̄n ∼ Ν(μ, σ2 / n)
As n grows, σ2/n → 0 (Strong Law of Large Numbers), concentrating all trajectories around μ.
