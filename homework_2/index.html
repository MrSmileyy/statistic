Theoretical Explanation: Stochastic Processes & Limit Theorems

1. Bernoulli vs. Rademacher Model

A discrete-time stochastic process is a sequence of i.i.d. (independent and identically distributed) random variables {Xt}t=1n.

  • Bernoulli Model: Each increment Xt takes values in {0, 1} with P(Xt = 1) = p and P(Xt = 0) = 1 - p. Moments are μ = p and σ2 = p(1 - p). In a security context, this represents the count of compromised servers or systems.
  • Rademacher Model: Each increment Xt takes values in {-1, +1} with P(Xt = 1) = p and P(Xt = -1) = 1 - p. In the symmetric case (p = 0.5), μ = 0 and σ2 = 1. In the asymmetric case, μ = 2p - 1 and σ2 = 4p(1 - p).

2. Partial Sum and Random Walk

The attacker's trajectory is described by the partial sum process:
S0 = 0,   Sn = ∑k=1n Xk.
By linearity of expectation and independence, E[Sn] = n ⋅ μ and Var(Sn) = n ⋅ σ2. Dispersion around the mean grows proportionally to √n.

3. Law of Large Numbers (LLN)

The Strong Law of Large Numbers states that the sample mean converges almost surely to the expected value:
Sn / n → μ   as n → ∞.
As n increases, the variance of the sample mean Var(Sn / n) = σ2 / n decays toward zero, yielding an increasingly concentrated distribution around μ.

4. Central Limit Theorem (CLT)

The Central Limit Theorem states that, regardless of the initial discrete distribution of Xk, the standardized random variable converges in distribution to a standard Normal:
Zn = (Sn - nμ) / (√n ⋅ σ) →d Ν(0, 1).
