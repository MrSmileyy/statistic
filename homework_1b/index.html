Foundations of Cryptanalysis: Caesar vs Elliptic Curves

1. The Chi-Squared Test (χ2)

The Chi-Squared statistic measures the weighted squared deviation between the observed empirical frequencies Oi in a candidate decrypted text and the expected theoretical frequencies Ei = N · pi for each letter i of the alphabet:

χ2(k) = ∑i ∈ {A..Z} [ (Oi - Ei)2 / Ei ]

Because the Caesar cipher is a deterministic cyclic permutation, the correct shift produces a χ2 value markedly lower than all other 24 incorrect shifts.

2. Effect of Text Length

By the Law of Large Numbers, the sample relative frequency converges to the theoretical probability as N → ∞. With very short texts (e.g., 10 characters), a single unusual letter (e.g., 'Z' or 'Q') causes a spike that misleads the test, generating false positives.

3. The Asymmetric Barrier of ECC

In EC-ElGamal, statistical attacks are defeated by the presence of the random nonce k: identical blocks produce different ciphertext points (probabilistic encryption indistinguishable under chosen-plaintext attack, IND-CPA). The only applicable attack on Toy ECC is an exhaustive search for the scalar d via iterative addition, made possible solely by the artificial restriction to 50,000 keys.
